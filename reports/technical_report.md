# PharmaStock AI Technical Report

## Executive summary

This project demonstrates two advisory inventory models: a 30-day product-level demand forecast and a batch-level expiry-risk flag. A Streamlit app loads the saved models to produce predictions and transparent stock guidance. The data and expiry outcomes are synthetic; measured performance must not be presented as validated pharmacy performance.

## Data and preparation

The fixed-seed generator creates 100 products, daily sales across 2024–2025, 300 batch records and monthly inventory snapshots. The expiry dataset contains 2,500 batch-snapshot rows across five snapshots. Labels simulate daily demand and allocate stock using first-expiry-first-out (FEFO); a batch is labeled positive when at least 10% of its stock remains unsold at expiry. The class distribution is 2,335 low-risk and 165 high-risk records (6.6% positive).

The historical files contain synthetic data only. Batch inventory holds initial quantities and expiry dates, not actual quantity-on-hand observations per snapshot. In the dashboard, the product-level snapshot stock is allocated over batches in proportion to batch quantities to enable a demonstration. This is an approximation and is prominently disclosed.

## Targets, features and leakage controls

Demand prediction unit is product × forecast origin; target is total sales in the next 30 days. Inputs are sales in the preceding 7/30/90 days, trailing daily average and standard deviation, month, category and product identity. Forecast origins are split chronologically into training, validation and test partitions. Inference uses only sales strictly before its forecast origin.

Expiry-risk prediction unit is a batch at an inventory snapshot. The target indicates whether at least 10% remains unsold at expiry in the simulated future. Features are current allocated stock, days to expiry, 30/90-day average demand, expected demand and stock-to-demand ratio, plus product identity. Batch ID is excluded. Entire snapshot months remain within one split: July–September train, October validation, November test. Threshold selection is performed using validation data. Because the target is generated by simulation, this does not establish independent real-world predictive validity.

## Models and evaluation

Demand compares a 30-day moving-average baseline with Random Forest and XGBoost candidates. The held-out results are:

| Model | MAE | RMSE | WAPE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Moving average | 68.34 | 106.95 | 9.93% |
| XGBoost | 28.24 | 37.27 | 4.10% |

Expiry compares a rule-based flag with a class-weighted Random Forest. The saved test metrics are:

| Model | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rule-based baseline | 0.923 | 0.316 | 0.471 |
| Random Forest | 0.594 | 0.500 | 0.543 |

The classifier catches a larger share of simulated risky batches than the baseline, with more false alarms. The test set has only 38 positive examples, so its precision/recall estimates have substantial sampling uncertainty. A single confusion matrix was not retained in the reports folder; the metrics above are copied from the saved comparison CSV.

## Inference and inventory guidance

`src/inference.py` loads the serialized artifacts, reconstructs one-hot encoded columns and returns actual model predictions. `app/dashboard.py` exposes these predictions for a selected synthetic product. Reorder point equals forecasted average daily demand × lead time plus estimated safety stock (1.65 × recent standard deviation × square root of lead time). Suggested quantity raises product stock to an estimated lead-time-plus-30-day target. Batch ordering uses FEFO. These are review prompts, not automatic purchase instructions.

## Error interpretation and influential inputs

The demand test MAE of 28.24 means the model's typical absolute error was about 28 units per product-origin sample in the held-out synthetic set. The saved feature-importance export ranks demand-history features among the learned inputs; importance is associative and does not prove causal influence. The expiry model's recall of 0.500 means it identified half of the positive test labels at its saved validation-selected threshold, leaving half undetected. Its precision of 0.594 means roughly 41% of its test flags were false positives under the synthetic labels.

## Reproducibility and quality checks

Use `requirements.txt`, regenerate the CSVs with the scripts, run the notebooks in order to recreate the model artifacts and metrics, launch `streamlit run app/dashboard.py`, and run `pytest -q`. Seeds are fixed at 42 in the existing generators and model training cells. Saved model files include the feature-column schema and the expiry threshold.

## Limitations and responsible use

The generated data has limited, hand-designed seasonality and no real-world representativeness. Expiry labels are derived from simulated future sales, and monthly snapshots are sparse. There is no independent external validation, calibration, real batch-level stock tracking, stockout censoring, promotions, returns, substitutions, supply disruption or procurement constraint modeling. High class imbalance and the small number of positive test samples make expiry metrics uncertain. The prototype must support a human inventory review, not make autonomous decisions. It must never be used as medication or treatment advice.
